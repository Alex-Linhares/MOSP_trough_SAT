\section{The statement in our Lean}
\label{sec:lean}
\begingroup\sloppy

Sections~\ref{sec:statement} and \ref{sec:proof} read OpenAI's statement
in OpenAI's files. This section describes the one thing we wrote in Lean
ourselves: a copy of the statement inside our own library, checked by
the same kernel, under our own name. The point is modest. Every sentence
this paper says about ``the verified statement'' is pinned to a named
object that Lean has accepted, and anyone who wants to use the statement
as an assumption later can cite that one name. Nothing is proved about
the hypothesis here, and nothing is claimed beyond what the copy and its
audit show.

Four words first, each in one sentence. In Lean a \emph{Prop} is a
sentence that may be true or false. A \emph{def} gives a sentence a name
without asserting it. A \emph{theorem} asserts a sentence and carries its
proof. An \emph{axiom} asserts a sentence without a proof. Our
restatement is a def: it names the sentence and asserts nothing.

The files are \repofile{lean/MOSPFormalization/SubexpPathwidth/PrimitiveMachine.lean}
and \repofile{lean/MOSPFormalization/SubexpPathwidth/Frame.lean}, written
on 2026-11-04 for an earlier study of ours on a different question. The
directory carries that study's name. The owner chose to leave the files
where they are rather than move them (\repofile{paper3/ETH_AMBIGUOUS.MD},
S3), and this paper cites nothing from that study: only the copied
model, the restatement, and six small lemmas about them, all listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:lean}.

\subsection{The copy: definitions only}
\label{sec:lean-copy}

The comparator file of family~378,
\clonefile{lean/ComparatorChallenges/SubexponentialSAT.lean}, has 220
lines. Lines 1--190 define the objects the theorem speaks about: the
formulas, their encoding as a string of bits, the multitape machine, its
step and its starting configuration, the exponent $\runtimeScale$ and
the bound built from it, the count word, and the bookkeeping that
records which tape cells a run has visited. Lines 191--219 state the
theorem \texttt{exact\_count\_and\_sat} and end it with \texttt{sorry},
the placeholder that a solution must replace. Line 220 closes the last namespace (a grouping of names).

Our \texttt{PrimitiveMachine.lean} is lines 1--190 and 220, and nothing
else. After a thirty-line header that records the source, the commit,
the file's hash and the Apache licence, the file is the copy
\leanref{copy-model}. The header says so, and a script checks it.
\repofile{paper3/scripts/lean_rediff.py} reads both files with
\texttt{open()} only, takes everything in ours from the line
\texttt{import Mathlib} on, and compares it byte for byte with the
clone's lines 1--190 followed by line 220. The diff is empty: 191 lines
on each side, identical. The script also checks the clone file's
SHA-256 against the hash the header records, and that the clone is at
commit \texttt{4eaee875}. The log is \repofile{paper3/lean/rediff.log}.

So the copy holds the theorem's \emph{vocabulary} and not the theorem.
The \texttt{sorry} at line 218 is outside the copied range, and our file
has no \texttt{sorry} and no axiom declaration outside its comments (the
same script counts them: zero and zero in each of our two files; the two
words do occur in comments, \texttt{PrimitiveMachine.lean} line 10 and
\texttt{Frame.lean} lines 13 and 83, saying that nothing is one). Everything in the
copy is a definition. A definition can be badly chosen, but it cannot be
false; what the definitions mean is Section~\ref{sec:statement}'s
subject, and the copy adds nothing to it.

\subsection{The restatement}
\label{sec:lean-restatement}

\texttt{Frame.lean} defines a sentence named \SubexpCount{}, with the
width $k$ as its argument:
\[
  \SubexpCount\ (k : \mathbb{N}) : \mathrm{Prop}.
\]
Its body is the body of the comparator theorem for width $k$. The
theorem's two leading parts are handled thus: $k$ is the argument of the
def, and the condition $2 \le k$ is carried by a second def,
\texttt{ExactCountAndSat}, which says ``for every $k \ge 2$,
\(\SubexpCount\ k\)'' (Appendix~\ref{app:lean}, \ref{lean:subexpcount}
and \ref{lean:exactcountandsat}). In words, \(\SubexpCount\ k\) is the
sentence of Section~\ref{sec:statement}: one fixed machine, on every
formula of width at most $k$, writes the exact number of satisfying
assignments, answers whether there is one, runs within the bound built
from $\runtimeScale$, and visits only polynomially many cells.

The same script checks that the body is the comparator's. It takes the
23 lines after \texttt{def SubexpCount (k : }$\mathbb{N}$\texttt{) : Prop :=}
in our file (lines 85--107). It takes the 23 lines after the theorem's
leading parts in the clone (lines 195--217, with the closing \texttt{:= by}
removed). Then it compares them in two ways: with every run of white space
collapsed to one space, and line by line with the indentation stripped.
Both comparisons find the two texts identical, 1,073 characters. The
only difference is that each of our lines starts two spaces further
left, because a def's body sits two columns inside a theorem's. We call
this ``character for character''; it is a textual match, by the same
method as Section~\ref{sec:proof}'s, and it rests on the file we copied
from being the comparator file at the commit we name.

Neither def is proved here, and neither is an axiom. The truth of
\(\SubexpCount\ k\) for $k \ge 2$ rests on OpenAI's proof and on
Sections~\ref{sec:proof} and \ref{sec:replay}, not on anything in our
library. Our library would be unchanged if the proof were wrong.

\subsection{What is proved, and what it means in words}
\label{sec:lean-lemmas}

Six small lemmas sit beside the defs, each proved in a few lines
(Appendix~\ref{app:lean}, \ref{lean:subexpcount-anti}--\ref{lean:trace-projection}).
None of them says anything about any machine's running time or about
whether the hypothesis is true.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A machine for width $k$ is a machine for every smaller
  width.} \texttt{SubexpCount.anti}: if \(\SubexpCount\ k\) holds and
  $k' \le k$ then \(\SubexpCount\ k'\) holds, with the same machine and
  the same constants, because a formula whose clauses have at most $k'$
  members has at most $k$. \texttt{ExactCountAndSat.subexpCount} unpacks
  the ``for every $k \ge 2$'' form.
\item \textbf{The bound is what it is.} \texttt{runtimeBound\_eq}: the
  wrapper \texttt{runtimeBound} $C\,A\,d\,L\,n$ unfolds, by definition,
  to $C\,(L+1)^d\,2^{A\cdot\rhon}$, the formula
  Section~\ref{sec:statement} reads. The proof is \texttt{rfl}: the two
  sides are the same term.
\item \textbf{The exponent grows more slowly than $n$.}
  \texttt{tendsto\_log\_sq\_div\_sqrt} says $(\ln y)^2/\sqrt{y} \to 0$
  as $y \to \infty$, and \texttt{runtimeScale\_isLittleO} says that
  $\rhon/n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, which is written $\rhon = o(n)$
  \leanref{rho-little-o}. This is the limit fact that
  Sections~\ref{sec:statement}, \ref{sec:beyond} and
  \ref{sec:consequences} cite: in the limit the exponent is smaller than
  $n$ by any factor one names. It is true at the same time as the
  title's fact that $\rhon > n$ for every $50 \le n < e^{5504}$, and the
  Lean says nothing about where the limit begins to bite.
  \texttt{isSubexpExponent\_runtimeScale} is the same statement for
  $A \cdot \rhon$, for every constant $A$ \leanref{is-subexp-exponent}.
\item \textbf{The time bound is a bound on the plain machine.}
  \texttt{Trace.config\_of\_locatedStep}: the comparator states its time bound on the \emph{located} step, which is the plain step plus the bookkeeping of visited cells beside each configuration. A run of the located step projects to a run of the
  plain step with the same number of steps, so the bound is a bound on
  the machine itself \leanref{trace-projection}. The one-line
  \texttt{LocatedCfg.monitor\_config} is its helper.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The axiom audit}
\label{sec:lean-audit}

Lean records, for every proved statement, the axioms its proof uses.
Three are standard in Mathlib and in every proof of
Sections~\ref{sec:proof} and \ref{sec:replay}: \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}. A fourth,
\texttt{sorryAx}, appears exactly when a proof was left out with
\texttt{sorry}. The audit \repofile{paper3/lean/axiom_check.lean} prints
the axioms of 21 declarations: the ten copied definitions the statement
rests on, the two defs, and the nine lemmas and helper definitions, in
the pattern of our first paper's audit. It is run from our library's
directory as
\begin{verbatim}
cd lean && lake env lean ../paper3/lean/axiom_check.lean \
  > ../paper3/lean/axiom_check.log
\end{verbatim}
and the log is committed. The result: nineteen lines on exactly the
three standard axioms; \texttt{Trace} on no axiom at all (it is defined by construction rules and carries no proof); \texttt{countWord} on two of the three (a proof may use fewer than the three, and fewer is never a concern); \texttt{sorryAx} on none. The library it runs against is built with
Lean~4.34.0-rc2 and Mathlib at \texttt{1192d624}, neither of which is
what the clone pins (Lean~4.34.1, Mathlib \texttt{d13f23b7},
Section~\ref{sec:proof}). That the copied model is accepted unchanged
by a different Lean version and a different Mathlib revision is small
evidence that it rests on nothing patched or private, and no more than
that. Our whole library builds with no \texttt{sorry} and no axiom
declaration, which the loop's gate checks after every session.

\subsection{What this does not claim}
\label{sec:lean-notclaim}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\(\SubexpCount\ k\) is a def of a Prop, never proved
  here.} Its truth is OpenAI's theorem, replayed in
  Section~\ref{sec:replay}; our library neither proves it nor assumes
  it. Nothing in this section makes the refutation more or less certain.
\item \textbf{The copy is definitions only.} The proof is not copied,
  and could not be: it is 3,091 modules (Section~\ref{sec:proof}). What
  is copied is the vocabulary of the statement, so that the restatement
  reads as the comparator does.
\item \textbf{No Lean computation evaluates the exponent.}
  $\runtimeScale$ is defined over the real numbers with real logarithms
  and is marked \texttt{noncomputable}; no \texttt{\#eval} can print
  $\rho(50)$ or $\rho(50)/50$. Every number in
  Section~\ref{sec:statement} comes from a Python script, not from
  Lean, and the Lean proves only the limit.
\item \textbf{The match is textual.} ``Character for character'' means
  the normalised texts are equal; it is not a check at the level of
  \texttt{lean4export}, and it rests on the clone being the repository
  at the commit named.
\item \textbf{The lemmas are trivial on purpose.} They fix the shape of
  the bound and the limit of the exponent, and nothing else. No theorem
  about any machine's running time, about the constants, or about the
  manuscript's sharper exponents is stated, because none is verified.
\item \textbf{The directory name is not a citation.} The files sit in a
  directory named for another study of ours; nothing from that study
  enters this paper, and the owner's decision to leave them in place is
  recorded, not argued.
\end{itemize}
