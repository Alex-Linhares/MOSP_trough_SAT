# Benchmark hunt: tracing instance sets through the experimental literature

*2026-09-30. Identification only: no instance file was downloaded. URLs marked
"200" were checked with `curl -I -L` on this date.*

**Method.** Pool = the 853 OpenAlex works citing the twelve Table 1 papers
(`paper1/data/openalex_citations.json`, abstracts re-fetched from OpenAlex) ∪ the
works kept by `paper1.relevance.kept_works` for the in-scope names (MOSP, gate
matrix layout, one-dimensional logic, PLA folding, interval thickness, node
search, vertex separation, pathwidth): 2,064 distinct works. A keyword pass
(problem term AND experimental term in title/abstract) gave 380 candidates;
these plus all non-pathwidth kept works were then judged by hand from title
and abstract. The 1,213 pathwidth works are overwhelmingly theory
(FPT/complexity); only those that actually compute pathwidth of instances
were kept. Instance sets were identified from abstracts, open-access full
texts (Resende's BRKGA preprint, Lima et al. arXiv 2409.04926, the SEA 2014
papers held in `literature/`, Martin et al. preprint), dataset READMEs, and
authors' repositories.

**Result: about 145 experimental works (130 table rows, some grouping
several works; duplicate records merged), 24 distinct instance collections.** Out of scope and not counted: gate matrix
*connection cost* (De Giovanni et al. 2013), cutting stock with limited open
stacks (CSP-LOS: Martin et al. 2022/2023, Guimarães 2021, etc.), talent
scheduling, tool switching, ordered cutting stock, and simple (two-per-track)
PLA folding. PLA works below are marked *simple* or *multiple* where the
abstract says so. Only multiple folding is in scope.

**Which are new.** The graph collections (rows 15–19, 23) and the published
MOSP matrix sets (rows 1–6) are what the other two agents are hunting. What
only this hunt surfaced:

- the **classic GMLP/LGAP netlists** on Lorena's INPE page (row 8). The live
  host is down, but the Wayback copy serves the files. This is the only
  public copy of the gate-matrix benchmark used from 1990 to 2016;
- the **Becceneri et al. 2004 set of 710** (row 7), used as recently as 2024
  but never published. It has to be requested from the UFOP/INPE group;
- the **IPC openstacks domain** (row 11): a MOSP benchmark from a different
  community, in PDDL, with thousands of solver runs on record;
- **MCNC/LGSynth PLA personality matrices** (row 12), the benchmark of the PLA
  folding literature;
- **NCI chemical graphs** (row 20) and **SDCC control-flow graphs** (row 21):
  real-world pathwidth instances from outside the layout community;
- the **PT-MOSP GitHub mirror** (row 24), which removes the ResearchGate
  dependence for the Carvalho–Soma and Frinhani sets.

## 3. Notes for the benchmark collection

1. The **GMLP netlists** (row 8) are the priority. They are small (11
   circuits, up to 141 gates × 202 nets) but they carry 25 years of
   best-known values across 12+ papers. They are MOSP instances with
   rows = nets, columns = gates.
2. **Becceneri 710** (row 7) is the last missing MOSP matrix set used in
   2024. Ask UFOP (Carvalho) for it.
3. **openstacks PDDL** (row 11) needs a converter: each problem lists
   `includes(order, product)` facts, which are the matrix. The IPC-2006
   generator was built on the Challenge instances, so some are likely
   duplicates of row 1. This needs checking.
4. **PLA** (row 12): only *multiple* folding is in scope. The MCNC files give
   the personality matrix, and multiple column folding of a PLA with c columns
   is GMLP on that matrix, so the files are usable as they stand.
5. The pathwidth-side real-world sets (NCI, SDCC CFGs) have very small
   pathwidth. They test the exact solvers' preprocessing, not their search.
