## 0. The idea, the object, and the stance

### 0.1 Where this comes from

Paper 3's useful product was one move: take a verified running-time bound
apart into its constants and ask at what input size the guarantee beats the
trivial one. The verified `#k`-SAT bound `C (L+1)^d 2^{A ρ(n)}` is above
`2^n` for every `50 ≤ n < e^{5504}` even at `A = 1` (the proof's `A ≥ 257`);
the manuscript's sharper route (Appendix D) has, at its fixed
`ε = 1/131072`, no recursion step below `2^131071` wires and beats `2^n`
only once `log s ≳ 1.5 × 10^12`. The owner's instruction (2026-11-06): make
that a **method**, apply it at scale, and produce paper 4 and a reusable
instrument.

### 0.2 The object: a bound taken apart

A **bound taken apart** is a record with five parts.

1. **The guarantee** as an explicit function of the input parameter(s):
   time (steps of a named machine model), space, or an approximation factor;
   with the parameter and the machine model named; a second parameter is
   fixed by a stated relation (`L ≈ 8knm` for the SAT encoding).
2. **Every constant with a status**, one of three:
   - **(a) computed** — read from the proof (paper 3's `A = 256 + A_t`);
   - **(b) existential** — "there exist `C, d`", never fixed; the
     comparison is then made **at constant 1, said so in every sentence**,
     with a **floor** recorded wherever the proof writes one (`A ≥ 257`,
     `C ≥ 28,701`), else "no floor";
   - **(c) explicit in the statement** (`2^{0.49n}`, `(L+2)^150020`,
     `ω < 2.371054886006746`).
3. **The baselines**, three: **brute force** with its constants written
   down (`2^n · poly` for SAT, `2^{n/2}` for Subset Sum, `O*(2^n)` for
   layouts); **the previous best bound**, with its own constant status; **the
   practical tool**, a measurement and not a bound (CaDiCaL, Blossom V, our
   `1.7 × 10^11` nodes at `n = 125`). The verdict is against brute force
   only.
4. **The crossover** `n*`: the smallest size at which the guarantee is
   below brute force (at constant 1 for (b); at the floor as a second row),
   with its digit count; **the cost at the crossover** `T(n*)` in digits of
   steps — the number paper 3 lacked a word for: family 124's polynomial
   beats `2^n` at about four million jobs, where both cost `10^{1,000,000}`
   steps; and the **feasible horizon** `n_f(B)`, the largest `n` at which
   the guarantee is under a budget `B` of `10^20` operations (a year of an
   exaflop machine, rounded) and `10^25`.
5. **The verdict**: **practical** (`n* ≤ n_f(10^20)`, constants (a) or
   (c)); **galactic** (`n*` beyond `n_f(10^25)` or above 25 digits, whatever
   the constants); **unknown-constants** ((b) with no floor and a feasible
   constant-1 crossover, so the true one could be anywhere); **no form**
   (the Lean fixes a `Polynomial ℕ` or `TM2ComputableInPolyTime` with no
   exponent: counted, listed, not computed).

**Prior art and what is new.** Lipton and Regan named the *galactic
algorithm* — wonderful asymptotically, never used to compute anything — in
*Gödel's Lost Letter*, 2010-10-23 (Regan's word, Lipton's concept; reprinted
in *People, Problems, and Proofs*, Springer 2013), with matrix
multiplication and Robertson–Seymour as examples; `reports/fpt_theory_practice_gap.md`
uses the word for Bodlaender–Kloks. New here is not the word but the
**systematic extraction** (every constant classified, with the line where
the proof fixes or fails to fix it), the **crossover and its cost as numbers
with digit counts**, the **horizon**, and the application to a
**machine-checked collection** where the bound's form is a Lean term. "We
found no prior statement" is bounded to the texts item 03 searches; "first"
is not used.

### 0.3 The stance

Paper 3's stance carries over: a careful reading, not a debunking. No proof
is read for correctness; "paper-only" never means "wrong"; a bound above
brute force says the *guarantee* is weak, never that the machine is slow;
every number has a regenerate command; every manuscript quotation carries
page and line of a committed `pdftotext` extract, every Lean quotation file
and line at clone commit `4eaee875`.

## 2. The instrument

`paper4/scripts/bound_reader.py` (a module with a CLI; tests in
`tests/test_bound_reader.py`; catalogue entries as `paper4/catalogue/*.toml`,
one per bound).

- **Input**: an entry with `guarantee` (an expression over the parameter
  and named constants, evaluated in **log₂ space** so that `n = 10^3000`
  never forms a float), each constant with `status`, `value` or `floor`, and
  `source` (file:line at `4eaee875`, or page:line of the extract); a
  `baseline` with the same fields; the parameter and any fixed relation; the
  budgets `[1e20, 1e25]`.
- **Output**: `n*` by bisection on `log₂ n` (exact big-integer check below
  `10^6`), its digits, `T(n*)` in digits, `n_f(B)`, the ratio at
  `[10, 10², 10³, 10⁴, 10⁶]`, rows at constant 1 and at the floor, the
  verdict by §0.2's rule, a CSV row for the table, and **the standard
  figure**: `log₂(guarantee / baseline)` against `log₁₀ n`, zero line =
  brute force, in `paper3/scripts/rho_over_n.py`'s conventions (serif 9 pt,
  palette `#2a78d6`/`#eb6834`/`#1baf7a`, ink `#0b0b0b`/`#52514e`, direct
  labels, practical range shaded, hollow landmark markers; the dataviz skill
  read before the first line of chart code).
- **Tests** (closed forms): `2^n` vs `2^n` (no crossover); `n^k` vs `2^n`
  against Lambert-W to `1e-9`; `c · 2^{0.49n}` vs `2^{n/2}` (`n* = 100 log₂
  c` exactly); paper 3's four numbers and Appendix D's two reproduced from
  their entries; `--check` fails if any quoted source line has moved.
- **Reading a Lean-stated bound mechanically — what is realistic.** The
  comparator terms have three shapes: `C * (len + k) ^ d` (124, 113, 103,
  115), `2 ^ (C * f(len))` (104, 378), and `Polynomial ℕ` /
  `TM2ComputableInPolyTime` (no form). `bound_reader.py lean-extract FILE`
  greps the definition named `timeBound|runtimeBound|polynomialClock|MainClaim|mixingTime`
  and its theorem, prints the term with line numbers, and classifies the
  shape by regex (`^ <literal>` explicit; `^ d` with `d` under `∃`
  existential; `Polynomial` no form). It does **not** parse Lean; the TOML
  transcription is by hand and `--check` pins it. A parser is out of scope.

## 4. Design decisions (fixed)

1. **The untrusted clone is data**: PDFs by `pdftotext` only into committed
   extracts under `paper4/extracts/`; Lean by `cat`/`sed -n`; scripts under
   `python3 -I` reading with `open()`; nothing built, run or imported — this
   loop needs no build, and the `bwrap` sandbox at `~/dev/untrusted/replay/`
   is the only place one could happen.
2. **Superscripts are read from the rendered page** (`pdftotext` turned
   `r^{2a}` into `r·2^a` in Appendix D): every verdict-deciding exponent,
   family 142's `10^12` above all, is confirmed on a `pdftoppm` rendering
   under `paper4/extracts/pages/`.
3. **The existential rule**: status (b) is compared at constant 1 with the
   words in the sentence; a floor gives a second row; no floor and a
   feasible constant-1 crossover is *unknown-constants*, never *practical*.
4. **Brute force is the verdict's baseline**, its constants written down.
5. **Nothing is run on any MOSP or pathwidth instance**; compute is the
   instrument's arithmetic.
6. **Subagents: at most 4 per session, one refuter, no fan-out.**
7. **No proof is read for correctness**, only for the lines that fix or
   bound constants.
8. **Every number has a regenerate command** under `paper4/scripts/`.

## 7. The risks

1. **Existential constants make a crossover meaningless** — §4.3's rule;
   the refuter files any crossover quoted without its status.
2. **`pdftotext` artifacts** (family 142's `10^12` arrived as three lines)
   — §4.2's rendered pages.
3. **The baseline is a judgement** — three per entry, the verdict against
   brute force only; a debatable brute force (104: pseudo-polynomial or
   exponential?) carries both and a `BOUNDS_AMBIGUOUS.MD` entry.
4. **A reading item does not find the constant** — the entry says "no floor
   on any page read", lists the pages, and the verdict follows the rule; one

## 8. What this plan does not claim

It does not claim a flaw in any manuscript or proof, a running time of any
machine, or a verdict on any algorithm's usefulness — only on what its
*guarantee* promises against trying everything. It asserts no theorem of
the collection; the companion Lean states crossover facts about explicit
functions and a monotonicity lemma. It runs no proved or open instance. It
claims, at the end, a method with a name and a rule, an instrument that
reproduces paper 3's numbers and applies to the next theorem in minutes, a
table over every algorithmic family of a machine-checked collection with
the constants' status on every row, four headline readings with their lines
quoted, and every unsettled question written down for the owner.
